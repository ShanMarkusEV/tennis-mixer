Privacy Policy

Effective 31 October 2026

10nis (at 10nis.win) helps you run social and competitive tennis sessions. This policy explains what data the app uses, why, and how you can remove it. Questions: shanmarkusdiablo@gmail.com.

Without an account

You can use the mixer without signing in. Your players, rounds and scores are then stored only in your browser on your device (local storage). We do not receive them.

When you sign in with Google

Sign-in uses Google. With your permission, Google shares your name, email address, profile photo and an account ID with us. We use them only to:

  • create your account and keep you signed in;
  • show your name and photo on your Account page;
  • link your session log to you.

We do not get your Google password, contacts, files or any other Google data.

Your session log

While you are signed in, each session you run is saved to your account: the date, format, number of courts, player names (and photo links for imported players), rounds and scores. Only you can see or change your sessions. This is enforced by the database, not just the app.

Importing players from Kuyy or Reclub

When you paste a Kuyy or Reclub activity link, our server reads that activity's publicly shown details: the participant list (names, profile photo links, whether they are confirmed or waitlisted, and their Kuyy or Reclub account ID and username when they have one) and the activity's date, venue and advertised level. It sends this to your device. The names end up in your roster, and if you are signed in, they are stored as described under "Players and levels" below. 10nis is not affiliated with Kuyy or Reclub.

Players and levels

When a signed-in organizer logs a session, each player on the roster is recorded in a player directory so their results can be tracked across sessions:

  • Players with a Kuyy or Reclub account are recorded once, by that account ID, so the same person on two organizers' rosters is one record. Their name and photo link come from the import.
  • Players typed by hand or without an account are recorded privately for that organizer only.
  • Match results (who played with and against whom, and the games won) are used to calculate a level score from 1 to 10, starting from the level the player chose, or for players who haven't chosen one, from the advertised level of imported activities.

Players linked to a 10nis account can see their own match history: for each match linked to them, the date, the organizer's name, the names of their partners and opponents, the score and how their level changed. They don't see the rest of the organizer's session.

Who can see a player's record and level: the player themselves, once linked to their 10nis account, and organizers who have that player on one of their rosters. There are no public profiles, and levels of players who haven't joined 10nis are never shown to anyone else.

Your profile photo

Linked players show the photo from their Google account. You can upload your own photo instead on the Account page; it is shrunk to a small square on your phone, then stored with Supabase. Like your Google photo, anyone who has its web address can view it. Choosing "Use Google photo" deletes the uploaded one, and so does deleting your account.

Your level and claiming your name

On the Account page you can choose your own level from 1 to 10. It is stored with your player record and is the starting point that your games move up or down.

An organizer can also share a link or QR code for a logged session. Anyone with the link can see that session's date and the names and photos on its roster. If you sign in there and tap your name, results under that name count towards your level. Share links expire after 30 days.

Organizers can scan a player's QR code with the camera inside 10nis. The camera image is read on the phone only to find the code; it is never sent or stored.

Each player also has a personal player code, shown with a QR code on the Account page. If you show it to an organizer, they can link a name on their roster to you directly. Anyone who has your code can see your name and photo and link you to a game, so share it only with organizers you play with.

Game PINs and notifications

An organizer can create a 6-digit game PIN for a logged session. Anyone who enters the PIN can see that session's title, place and time, the names and photos on its roster, the matches and the scores, while the session is active (until two days after its last change) or until the organizer turns the PIN off. Following a game needs no account: the name you pick is remembered only on your phone. To stop people guessing PINs, when a wrong PIN is entered we keep the network (IP) address it came from for up to an hour; an address with many wrong PINs is paused for a few minutes. Correct PINs are not recorded.

If you turn on notifications, we store your browser's push address with the game and the name you follow, so we can tell your phone when there's a new round. The notification itself carries no data; your phone fetches the game to word it. The push address is deleted when the organizer turns the PIN off, when you follow another game or turn notifications off, or when your browser says it's no longer valid. Notifications are delivered by your browser's push service (Google, Apple, Mozilla or Microsoft).

The 10nis Threads account

The official 10nis account on Threads uses Meta's Threads API to find public posts about playing tennis (for example people looking for a tennis partner) and comments on its own posts, and to draft short replies with an AI model (Anthropic's Claude). A person reviews and approves each reply before it is posted, except replies to comments on 10nis's own posts. We keep the text and username of those public posts for up to 30 days, only to review and send replies. If you don't want 10nis to reply to you, tell us or block the account.

Other people's names

The players you add are usually other people. Please only add people who are part of your session. If someone asks you to remove their name, you can rename or delete them in the app, or delete the session. They can also contact us.

Anonymous usage counts

To know how many people use 10nis, including people who don't sign in, the app keeps a random code on your device and records a few events with it: opening the app (once a day), generating a round, finishing a session, importing players (and whether from Kuyy or Reclub), joining a game by PIN, signing in, and adding 10nis to your home screen. Each event stores only that code, the event name, whether you were signed in, the app version, whether you use 10nis from your home screen, and small details like the number of rounds. It doesn't include your name, account, IP address or anything you typed, and we only look at totals. Clearing the site's data in your browser gives you a new code.

Reporting a problem

If you use "Report a problem", we receive your message and any contact details you add, your account if you're signed in, and some context to help us find the cause: the app version, the screen you were on, your browser, whether you use the home-screen app, and the last few errors the app ran into. We use this only to fix problems and reply to you.

What we don't do

  • No ads, and we never sell or rent your data.
  • No advertising, no tracking cookies and no third-party analytics. We only keep the anonymous usage counts described below. Google's sign-in button may set its own cookies, under Google's privacy policy.
  • We don't share your data with anyone except the service providers below, which run the app for us.

Service providers

  • Supabase: sign-in and database. Data is stored in Singapore.
  • Cloudflare: hosts the website and runs the Kuyy/Reclub import.
  • Google: Sign in with Google.
  • Google Fonts and jsDelivr: deliver the app's font and code libraries. They see your IP address when loading them.
  • Kuyy and Reclub: photos of imported players load directly from their servers, which see your IP address when loading them.

These providers may keep short-lived technical logs, such as IP addresses and request times, to operate and secure their services.

Google user data

10nis's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. We use Google data only to provide sign-in and the features described above, not for advertising, and no person reads it except when you ask us for help or the law requires it.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • A session: tap × next to it in your Account's session log.
  • Your whole account: tap Delete account on the Account page. Your account, profile, all sessions you logged and the level you chose are deleted immediately and permanently. Results from sessions other organizers logged stay in their session logs, under the name they used, no longer connected to you.
  • Data on your device: use Reset everything on the Players page, or clear the site's data in your browser.

We keep account data until you delete it. You can also email us to access, correct or delete your data.

Children

10nis is not meant for children under 13, and we don't knowingly collect their data.

Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update this page and its effective date. Significant changes will be shown in the app.
